Project ideas

Every project idea the log has written, pulled out of the reports and lined up on one page — what it is, the smallest first step, and the day it was proposed. Ideas repeat, sharpen and die here exactly as they did in print.

1 November 2026

  • An overnight IMU drift watchdog

    A $5 IMU lies: after a week on a shelf, its bias has moved. The fix is a 30-second nightly static self-check — park the rover on blocks, log zero-rate output, alert when the bias passes a threshold, so you re-calibrate before a field day. Smallest step: one MPU-6500 overnight log and a bias-velocity threshold you can defend.

  • A photodiode entropy wall

    A photodiode sampled by a Pico at audio rates, a running min-entropy estimate per second, and the live SHA-256 digest of the noise stream shown as a shifting glyph wall on a small OLED — a lava lamp you can prove is random. Smallest step: sample the diode, estimate the entropy of its intervals.

  • An overnight RTL-SDR satellite pass logger

    A $20 RTL-SDR with a Pi and a script that walks the TLEs, wakes for each NOAA pass at 137 MHz, records it and decodes the APT signal into one daily image. Cheap space engineering, entirely offline. Smallest step: schedule one predicted pass tonight, produce a legible image by breakfast.

31 October 2026

  • An open dictionary for e-bike and e-scooter CAN buses

    A €10 CAN-FD adapter, one commute of logged traffic, then name every message ID you can identify and publish the mapping with a small open dashboard. Cheap micromobility is everywhere and its buses are undocumented. Smallest step: log one ride, cluster frames by ID and cadence, and write down what you can prove.

  • A campus barometer constellation

    Three or four BMP390 sensors on ESP32s logging pressure every second to a shared page; the goal is catching squall passages and roof-wave pressure drops from differential timing between nodes. Weather stations are sparse; pressure *gradients* are invisible. Smallest step: one node logging 24 h, then correlate pressure velocity against the observed wind.

  • An audition-mode metronome

    A small web metronome where beats deliberately drop out on a configurable curve — the way orchestra panels start the click and silence it, trusting the player to hold the tempo. The tool tests whether you can. Smallest step: a 60-beat pattern with a recede curve, WebAudio, no dependencies.

30 October 2026

  • A battery flight-log standard

    Log every LiPo pack's internal resistance, discharge curves and crash history in a small append-only file, then fit a retirement predictor per pack — packs are robotics' priciest consumable and their logs live in notebooks. Smallest step: a CSV schema plus a discharge→IR extractor from one bench run.

  • An offline voice vocabulary for a rover

    ~20 command words with a wake word, running entirely on an ESP32-S3's vector extensions — no cloud, no SBC. Smallest step: record the vocabulary, train a micro-speech model, measure false-wakes.

  • A deterministic replay format for jam games

    JSONL input logs + a fixed-seed PRNG, so a jam entry can ship a replay judges watch without running the binary. Smallest step: a replay wrapper around one game's input loop, plus a hash check.

27 October 2026

  • An annotated S-1 reader

    pull the filing's HTML, mark every risk-factor disclosure, and chart the counterparty commitments — the year's most-read filing is being skimmed, not parsed. Why now: it is the reference document on AI economics for months. Smallest step: the SEC HTML plus a tag pass on the risk section.

  • Hash-chained agent logs

    instrument a small agent with invariants and append-only audit trails so that "the agent hid its actions" becomes a testable claim, not a vendor's sentence. This week's filings and reports make the ground richer than when the idea first appeared. Smallest step: JSONL log + hash chain + three tamper tests in Python.

  • A weekend wall-clone of the RCR challenge

    take the toy above seriously — replace hand-coded holds with an ordered hold-graph and a real reachability algorithm, then attach a cheap webcam pose-estimator. Smallest step: hold-graph from the toy's layout plus a reachability BFS that colours available moves.

Smallest step: hash-chain a JSONL action log in Python and write three tamper tests. This is the Outlook's advice turned into a weekend artefact.
