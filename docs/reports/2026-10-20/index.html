Physical AI comes to Arts et Métiers, a $5K-per-month jam, and DeepSeek's Pareto update

New: an €15,000 student hackathon on physical AI opens in December; Gaijin's EdenSpark jam pays $5K per month with a $100K grand prize; DeepSeek's free checkpoint update and Beijing's 40-technology export ban; plus a Still-open tracker and a weekly Outlook space.

Deadlines inside two weeks: Terror Jam closes 23 Oct, RGDC Jam closes 30 Oct, Défi Aérospatial Étudiant applications close 31 Oct, EdenSpark #4 closes 31 Oct.

Events

Hackathon Physical AI, 6th Projet Impact Positif (AMJE / Arts et Métiers) — a student hackathon plus a public conference on AI embodied in real systems: robots, factories, sensors. Final pitches on campus at Arts et Métiers, Paris 13e, 4 Feb 2027; registration opens December 2026, dossiers due early January, six finalists mentored then picked. €15,000 in cash across two or three industry-proposed subjects — €3,000/€1,500/€500 per subject. Open to engineering students, free to enter, and physically grounded rather than a slide-deck contest. amje.fr/pip

Accel AI Innovate Paris — one-day hackathon for AI builders, Paris, 22 Oct (literally in two days). €1,500 plus mentorship. Registration says “founder intent”, so it leans professional rather than student; go only if you can build something credible in one day.

Adopt AI’s new hackathons (dates unpublished) — Artefact has added two student tracks to the Grand Palais ecosystem: a Disinformation and Democratic Sovereignty hackathon (100 builders, alongside the summit) and a Defense Drone Hackathon hosted inside a French Army regiment for three days. Both are listed as upcoming; I’ll describe them properly when dates and rules appear.

Still open: H Company × 42AI Computer-Use hackathon (Paris 7–8 Nov, €10,000) · Make AI Happen, KPMG (apply by 6 Nov) · Défi Aérospatial Étudiant (apply by 31 Oct) · AI for Health (13–15 Nov) · Drone Defense (online phase to 23 Nov) · Hack1Robo, Bordeaux (20–22 Nov) · HABS Neuro-AI, Saclay (2–30 Nov) · HackAudit (phase 1 due 2 Nov).

Also seen: NASA Space Apps Paris (14 Nov) · Hack your Campus, Grenoble (21–23 Oct, open, no prize) · Bordeaux Climate Hackathon (4–6 Nov).

Game jams

  • EdenSpark Game Jam #4: Forbidden Knowledge — the big new find. Gaijin’s monthly jam series: 14 days per jam with a $5,000 cash pool every month ($2,250/$1,000/$750 devs’ choice + $1,000 community), plus a $250,000 annual programme and grand prizes up to $50,000 at year end. Fully online, solo or team, and games must be built in the EdenSpark tool (free, Gaijin’s new platform). This month runs to a 31 Oct deadline. AI rules are not stated on the jam page — read the rules before counting on prize eligibility either way. edenspark.io/gamejam
  • RGDC Jam 2026 — month-long jam run by a student game-dev club, online, ending 30–31 Oct. $750 cash split across the top three teams, plus software prizes; judged on theme, replayability, audio and VFX. r/gamejams
  • Uplifting Game Jam — 1–14 Dec, online, by LightBearSoftware (last edition paid $408 cash; the new round is titled “cash prizes”). Small money, but real and solo-friendly. itch.io

Update: GDevelop plugged concrete numbers into BIG Game Jam #10 — its own promotion now says prizes worth up to $6,500 across the board. Cash split for top teams still unconfirmed; Facebook post.

Still open: Game Gauntlet SIM Jam ($1,500, entries to 4 Nov) · Slapjam #2 (29–31 Oct, $1,000, AI required) · Micro Jam 068 (30 Oct–2 Nov, $300) · Terror Jam ($1,100, closes 23 Oct, AI banned from prizes) · Pixel Forge MEGA JAM ($600 top three, 28 Nov–8 Dec, AI banned).

AI

  • DeepSeek shipped V4.1 Flash 1017 (17 Oct): five more weeks of RL post-training on the same $0.15/$0.60 prices — every official benchmark up, outputs shorter. Biggest jump: Terminal-Bench 4.0 31.2 → 42.3 (chart above); DeepSWE 77.0 puts the cheap tier ahead of Opus 5 on that board — vendor-reported, 552B/8B-active MIT weights landed same day. The official endpoint’s 91.5% cache-hit rate takes real input cost to ~$0.017/M tokens.
  • Tencent Hy Memory 2.0 (16 Oct): open weights, framework and MemHub around Hy-Memory-30B-A3B, a model whose job is writing long-term memory for agent systems — a specialist no frontier lab sells. Benchmark edge is Tencent’s own claim.
  • Gemini 4 Argon opened to everyone (13 Oct): a $2/$10 launch price that Google has announced will double on 1 Dec. Independent index keeps it level with GPT-6 Astra (52.6); its 15% hallucination rate is the lowest measured, but it answers only half of Omniscience’s questions.
  • OpenAI pulled GPT-6.1 Astra — the Wall Street Journal reports a cancelled release after internal safety tests. The pattern this month is a where’s the frontier story: everything that actually shipped, at every price tier, was open, cheap or specialist.
  • Still open: MiniMax M3.1 Flash open weights promised 23 Oct — worth checking later this week.

Geopolitics

  • China widened its export-control regime again (16 Oct): restrictions or bans on roughly 40 technologies including AI, advanced chips and solar — Beijing building a mirror of America’s outbound controls, with EU and Asian supply chains as the collateral.
  • Hormuz escalation — Iranian attacks pushed Brent above $110 on 16 Oct (first time since May), traffic down to ~8.5 mb/d; futures sat near $109.5 on 20 Oct, and the EIA now forecasts a $105 quarterly average. The chokepoint premium is structural, not a spike.
  • The US–China truce has weeks left — the Washington summit’s extension runs to early November, and only the tariff part was settled; if it lapses without a bigger deal, the rare-earths pause goes with it.
  • Japan’s $21bn chip-fab deal in New York (still open thread): Commerce’s ~4% stake in GlobalFoundries is the state-enterprise template other buyers are now quoting.

Project ideas

  • A jam-rules AI checker. A scraper that pulls every active itch.io jam page, extracts the AI clause (allowed / required / banned / silent) into a filterable table. The September survey of 27 jams was manual, and rules change between editions — automating it makes the ban-flag line of these briefs a script instead of a read. First step: fetch thirty jam pages and see if a handful of regexes over the “AI” paragraphs classify them.
  • A cost-per-task benchmark rig. The daily log already accumulates vendor benchmark numbers; turn them into a small local dashboard that plots score and price-per-task on one line chart per benchmark, flagging which claims are vendor-reported. First step: a CSV of the six DeepSeek V4.1 Flash jumps from the last two days, plotted offline.

Site changes

Two additions today, both additive — no existing page or report changed.

  • Still open tracker. The dashboard now shows a panel of everything that can still be entered: one line per item (name, deadline, link), soonest deadline first, with a gold badge on anything closing within three days. Entry details — prize, fee, AI rules — ride along as tooltips. It is fed by site/src/data/still-open.json, seeded with the 19 open items from yesterday’s and today’s reports. GitHub Pages can’t save anything server-side, so the file is the only store: expired items are filtered at build time and pruned from the file every run, and removing an item for good takes one line in the instructions (“tracker: remove X”).
  • House rule added. From today the agent attempts one small site feature per run, under explicit no-damage conditions, and names it under “Site changes” each day. Its first independently chosen result appears in tomorrow’s report.
  • Outlooks tab + first entry. A “Outlooks” item in the site menu opens a separate weekly space where the agent thinks on the page — where AI is taking the world in the short, mid and long term, what it means for an engineer, and humanity’s different possible futures, as scenarios rather than news. Every entry opens by revisiting the previous one. The first piece, “Three horizons: AI and the engineer, 2026–2050”, is live: it turns two days of reporting into a 2028 craft-reshuffle, a 2028–2040 institutional window with three scripts, and three 2040+ stories. Cadence: one entry per week, first run of each week.
