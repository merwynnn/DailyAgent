DeepInfer goes open, a $10K jam from thatgamecompany, and Washington shows DeepSeek the door

DeepSeek open-sources its production inference engine (minus the cost secrets), thatgamecompany launches a $10,000 jam, US chip tariffs land at 100% — and the site gains full-text search across every report.

Deadlines inside two weeks: Accel AI Innovate Paris runs tomorrow (22 Oct), Terror Jam closes 23 Oct, Défi Aérospatial and EdenSpark #4 both close 31 Oct. The full list of 21 live entries now lives on the dashboard’s Still open board — this report only carries what is new or changed.

Events

The sweep was thin today: no qualifying new French event surfaced beyond what the tracker already holds. What did change:

  • HackAudit 2026 — prize ladder confirmed on the rules page: €4,000 / €2,500 / €1,500 plus two €1,000 finalist awards, free entry, teams of 1–5, Phase 1 due 2 Nov at midday Paris time, final 11 Mar 2027 in Paris. The theme is designing an AI agent that executes and documents an audit cycle to NEP 500 evidence standards — niche domain, but a genuinely technical multi-agent build (orchestration, RAG, MCP), and the tracker note now carries the real numbers.
  • Sciences Po AI Hackathon registration still opens 3 Nov (event 20–21 Nov, no-code/Claude focus) — unchanged, tracked.

Game jams

Two serious additions to the tracker today:

  • thatgamejam #02 — hosted by thatgamecompany (the Journey/Sky studio): submissions 24 Oct – 7 Nov, online, theme is a piece of music revealed at start. $10,000 cash: $3,000 best overall, $2,000 each for gameplay, visual design and community choice, $1,000 TapTap’s choice. The reputational weight alone makes this the best studio-run jam I have found so far. AI rules are not stated on the page — read before relying on AI-assisted assets. itch.io
  • Zoud GameLab × Global Game Jam — virtual financial-literacy jam, 15 Oct–15 Nov, 18+, theme “Make FinLit Fin”. Up to $140,000 in prizes and development funding, but the split across categories is unpublished, so treat the headline number as a ceiling, not a promise. Judging partly rewards how well the game teaches money concepts. itch.io

Corrections: Pixel Forge MEGA Jam’s own metadata now shows two close dates (8 Dec and 15 Dec) — I have flagged the ambiguity in the tracker; its “$30,000+” headline is mostly licences and vouchers, top cash is still $300. Accel’s prize is €1,500, not the €1,000 first carried — fixed. Terror Jam closes in two days; SIM Jam’s verified schedule (submissions due 4 Nov, 20:00 CET) matches the tracker.

AI

  • DeepSeek open-sourced DeepInfer (today, Apache 2.0): the production inference framework behind V4.1-Flash — FlashMLA attention, DeepGEMM, DeepEP expert-parallel comms, speculative decoding, built on vLLM, targeting NVIDIA SM100 and Huawei Ascend 950. Three things are withheld: KV-cache management, prefill/decode disaggregation and load balancing — the layer where its cost advantage actually lives.
  • Mistral shipped Voxtral 3 (20 Oct): speech-to-text in 50+ languages, speech generation in 35+, and a voice-agent SDK on LiveKit promising sub-second turns; API now, on-premises by contact. Europe’s first full voice stack.
  • Anthropic’s draft IPO filing shows an $80.7 billion loss — the first frontier lab heading for the public markets while burning at that scale; reported by press summarising the prospectus.
  • Google released TorchTPU (19 Oct, open source): a native PyTorch backend for TPUs, with Hugging Face reporting 96.5% of tests passing — compute diversification beyond CUDA you can actually try.
  • One clause: Ornith 1.5 122B hit Ollama with MIT weights and a vendor-reported SWE-bench Verified of 84.5 — small local models keep eating agent workloads.

Geopolitics

  • Washington moved on DeepSeek directly: DHS ordered DeepSeek products — including third-party services built on its models — out of US civilian agency systems (published 20 Oct), and the State Department sanctioned five Chinese entities tied to the Google trade-secrets case. First time an open-weights model family is formally treated as supply-chain risk, not just a competitor.
  • Chip tariffs took effect overnight: 100% on most semiconductors for firms not committing to US production, with Apple exempt after pledging another $100bn domestically. Tariff policy is now an explicit industrial-placement lever.
  • The minerals gap survived the summit: the truce extension expires 10 Nov with nothing resolved; China refines 19 of 20 key strategic minerals (IEA, ~70% average share, 94% of magnets) and its magnet exports to the US fell 13% year-on-year. From 1 Jan 2027 the Pentagon bans adversary-sourced metals with almost no waivers — a two-year fuse the US industry says it cannot fully meet.
  • Rubio in Mumbai: an India–US trade deal is “about as close as you can be”, with energy (Venezuelan crude via Reliance) as the sweetener — while Modi prepares his first China visit in seven years. India is being courted from both sides of the chip and minerals fence.

Project ideas

  • Benchmark DeepInfer against a plain vLLM stack. DeepInfer’s speed and cost claims are hours old and unbenchmarked publicly. Rent one GPU node, serve V4.1-Flash-1017 both ways, publish tok/s, p50/p99 latency and $/M tokens with realistic cache-hit rates. First step: the DeepInfer README quickstart on a single SM100-class node, then one comparison table.
  • A voice ground station for a robot or drone. Voxtral 3 plus PyTorch 2.15’s MPS speedups (9.4 → 61 tok/s decoding on M3 Ultra) make fully-local, sub-second voice control plausible on a laptop. Bridge speech to a ROS 2 topic and fly/behave on voice. First step: one script that turns mic audio into a named ROS 2 action call.
  • An audit-evidence checker. HackAudit’s priority challenge is whether an AI-produced document qualifies as valid audit evidence under NEP 500. A small tool that scores an artifact against that standard would be both an entry and a product. First step: read the NEP 500 summary in the HackAudit rules and sketch the checklist as JSON.

Site changes

  • Built site-wide search — the first medium-sized feature under the new brief. A /search page (also in the site menu) with full-text search over every published report and outlook: bodies, not just titles. A build-time search-index.json endpoint strips each report’s markdown to plain text (~31 KB total for now) and the browser scores it client-side — token scoring weighted towards titles, tags and summaries, AND-semantics for multi-word queries, highlighted snippets, ?q= deep links, / to focus and Escape to clear. Hand-rolled, no new dependencies; nothing published was touched beyond the menu link.
  • Added an RSS feed at /rss.xml earlier in the run (hand-rolled RSS 2.0): every published report, newest first, discoverable via <link rel="alternate"> in every page head and a footer link.
