The Army wants quantum ideas, an Amiga jam, and Russian oil suddenly obeys

The Agence Innovation Défense is dishing out €12,000 for three days of quantum-collaboration defence hacking at the Grand Palais (registration status: confusing), OpenAI retires its first-generation models today, and Russian-oil sanctions drew a 5% price spike — and real compliance from China and India.

Deadlines inside two weeks: Terror Jam closes tonight. The Bordeaux climbing-robot workshop starts next Wednesday (29 Oct) and registration has no published cut-off — email now if it’s of interest. Four deadlines land on 31 Oct: Défi Aérospatial Étudiant, EdenSpark #4, RGDC Jam and Slapjam #2.

Events

  • Hackathon Quantique Défense (Agence Innovation Défense × Artefact) — the French defence innovation agency’s successor to last year’s 300-participant drone hackathon, this time built around quantum computing: 4 service challenges (Armée de Terre, Marine, Armée de l’Air et de l’Espace, Interarmées) worth €12,000 (4 × €3,000), teams of 4–8, three competition days at the Grand Palais, Paris, 2–4 December, embedded in the Adopt AI summit. Target audience is explicit: engineering students, PhD students and doctoral candidates. Quantum compute time is provided — GENCI’s Atos emulator plus Pasqal/Quandela machines, with discussions ongoing with OVHcloud and other builders — and the published themes are genuinely rich: QUBO logistics optimisation, attacking post-quantum cryptography, fusing multisensor detectors, GPS-denied navigation, weak-signal extraction, high-dimensional design optimisation. One honest wrinkle: the June announcement said registration ran to 4 October, but the Agorize page still shows a live sign-up. The tracker carries it with a 31 Oct check-back marker — email the AID contact before treating it as enterable. The same article confirms two more AID × Artefact hackathons are being prepared — presumably the tracked Drone Defense Hackathon (24–26 Nov) and one more.

The rest of the sweep was quiet: nothing new that clears the bar surfaced for the second straight day.

Game jams

  • Amiga Tools Jam 2026 — closes 26 Nov, voting to 24 Dec, cash for the top three voted by the community (amounts unstated). Niche and retro, but tools-for-Amiga is programming through and through; flagged as amounts-unknown in the tracker.
  • The official Unity Game Jam (5–13 Nov) is on, but its prize line is community rating and visibility, not cash — mentioned once and left there.
  • Terror Jam ends tonight. Two October-jam nights left in the month: Slapjam #2 (29–31 Oct, AI required) and EdenSpark #4.

AI

  • OpenAI retires its first generation: gpt-3.5-turbo and the remaining o-series models are scheduled to shut down today (DALL·E already went in May). The reasoning-model era’s founding models are decommissioned a year into their successors.
  • MiniMax M3.1 Flash weights were slated for today; at run time they are not yet confirmed live (API and free OpenRouter endpoint are). The 42 on the Artificial Analysis index is on record from Monday, and nobody has independently tested the “93% hallucination rate” claim yet.
  • Unitree by the numbers (22 Oct, vendor-reported): 10,000–20,000 cumulative G1 humanoid units since May 2024 at ¥99,000, 23–43 joint motors, ~2 h runtime — the claim is that it is the world’s best-selling humanoid.
  • Barclays will cut ~5,000 roles by year-end — about 5% of its ~93,000 workforce — citing multi-year technology investment and AI adoption reducing developer and support demand; 1,000 posts already closed in H1, with Q3 profit up 12%. A concrete future-of-work datapoint, not a forecast.
  • An AI-assisted proof of the 31-year-old KLS conjecture (21 Oct): a 140-page bounding of the Kannan–Lovász–Simonovits constant to O(1), via a human-directed agentic workflow that explored 100+ approaches — with the authors noting unguided attempts by frontier models failed. Not peer-reviewed, but a real marker for AI in mathematics.

Geopolitics

  • Russian-oil sanctions are suddenly biting: prices spiked ~5% on 23 Oct, and China’s state oil firms plus a major Indian refiner halted Russian crude imports almost immediately. Enforcement credibility, not just tariff headlines.
  • Zelenskyy told the European Council on 23 October not to ease sanctions (“lifting them will make Putin stronger”) right as the Brussels meeting weighs the support track.
  • Putin says Russia is “ready to negotiate”, and per Reuters agreed on 21 Oct to resume three-way talks — but the military-objective line from Moscow is unchanged, so treat the peace track as noise until the conditions move.
  • Europe is diversifying in both directions in one week: MEPs travel to China next week for trade talks, and EU–GCC leaders meet tomorrow in Saudi Arabia on trade, energy and security.

Project ideas

  • A QUBO playground aimed at the quantum hackathon themes. The nine themes are public; even if registration turns out closed, a small portfolio demo of “route planning under threat as QUBO” (risk-weighted shortest path on a grid, annealed against A*) speaks the AID’s language for future challenges. Why now: themes published, event 2–4 Dec. Smallest step: QUBO-ise a 5×5 grid path and solve it with simulated annealing.
  • The M3.1 Flash local bench, sharpened: the weights date has actually arrived. The first hours after a hot release are the right time to publish tokens/s numbers on real consumer hardware. Smallest step: GGUF conversion + one prompt set.
  • A two-dimensional humanoid on stairs, from scratch: port the RCR climbing idea into a MuJoCo/Pinocchio toy — a 2D biped walking a stepped profile with contact planning. Smallest step: reproduce a flat-ground walk with a stock humanoid model, then add one step.
