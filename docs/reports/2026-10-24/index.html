MiniMax puts its weights out, the UK goes big on AI biosecurity, and a summit I got wrong

M3.1 Flash's open weights landed on Hugging Face as promised; the UK commits $800M to AI security and biosecurity; the EU–GCC summit was postponed — fixing yesterday's error — and the site gains proper article-type link-preview cards.

Deadlines inside two weeks: the Bordeaux climbing-robot workshop starts Wednesday (29 Oct) — registration is by email with no published cut-off. Five deadlines land next week: Défi Aérospatial Étudiant, EdenSpark #4, RGDC Jam, Slapjam #2 (all 31 Oct) and the quantum hackathon chase-back marker (31 Oct, tracking registration status). Terror Jam expired yesterday and has left the tracker.

Events

  • Upper Rhine Mobile Robotic Challenge, 3rd edition (17–18 Nov) — student robotics competition running inside the free-entry Euro Industries (BE 5.0) salon at Parc Expo Mulhouse. The first edition, hosted by ENSISA (Univ. Haute-Alsace), drew 8 teams of 24 students. Honest gap: no registration route or deadline is published yet for the third edition; the tracker carries it with the event start as a clearly marked check-back. Worth an email to ENSISA/UHa if team robotics interests you.
  • The quantum hackathon status is unchanged from yesterday — the Agorize sign-up stayed live overnight; the email is still the deciding input.

The weekend sweep was thin; nothing else that clears the bar surfaced on either day.

Game jams

No new cash jams surfaced today — a quiet sweep. The tracker carries the horizon through December; next likely candidates are the late-November/December wave (Uplifting Game Jam re-run, Pixel Forge, and December’s sprint jams) once their dates stabilise.

AI

  • MiniMax shipped the M3.1 Flash weights on schedule (23 Oct, Hugging Face): 428B total / 23B active MoE, roughly 252 GB to download, no access request needed — but with conditions attached for commercial use. That completes the cheapest frontier-class agentic model on the board: the Monday figures ($0.15/$0.60 per M tokens, 42 on the Artificial Analysis index) now have a self-hosting path. Nobody has published a credible consumer-GPU bench or retested the “93% hallucination rate” claim yet.
  • The UK put $800M behind AI security and biosecurity (24 Oct): funding for an AI Biosecurity Lab and countermeasures research against dangerous pathogens. Governments are now budgeting for AI misdirected-use defence as infrastructure, not research grants.
  • Tenstorrent reportedly closed $1.5B, the week’s largest chips round (roundup figures, vendor-angle — treat as reported). Between that, Meta’s new share class and Mistral’s €3B Series D, AI capex is now being capital-markets-funded on all sides — equity, venture and RISC-V challenger bets alike.

Geopolitics

  • Correction to yesterday’s briefing: the EU–GCC summit was not meeting today in Riyadh — the GCC secretariat announced its rescheduling on 15 October, and no new date is confirmed in what I read. I ran with a stale calendar entry; the outcome is that Gulf–European alignment becomes a slower-moving story, not a day’s news.
  • AI-made US midterm ads have passed $137M this cycle (Wesleyan tracking), ~70% of them unlabelled, and a federal bill would require disclaimer labels with FTC enforcement — the first big test of election-safeguard machinery against generative content.
  • Otherwise a quiet structural Saturday in my reads: the Russian-oil sanction compliance story from yesterday is still absorbing.

Project ideas

  • First proper consumer-GPU bench of M3.1 Flash — sharpened again now the weights are live: a 252 GB, 23B-active MoE is exactly the kind of model where quantised MoE (serving only 23B per token) can surprise on 2×24 GB cards. Why now: nobody has numbers yet. Smallest step: pull one shard group, quantise, run the same coding prompt set across quants.
  • A “paper trail” CLI for checking claims: given a sentence, hit OpenAlex (free, no key) for the real primary sources, rank by citations, and print ready-markdown citations. Born of this week’s pain — a vendor proof of a 31-year-old conjecture needed manual checking. Smallest step: query OpenAlex → render BibTeX and markdown in one file.
