Cheaper in the cloud only: M3.1 Flash's real local story, and the strikes behind a summit

vLLM's day-0 serving of M3.1 Flash shows 418–725 tok/s per user on datacenter GPUs — while the consumer-GPU hope dies at ~249 GB; Houthi strikes on Aramco infrastructure explain Friday's postponed EU–GCC summit; the 404 page gains a working search.

Deadlines inside two weeks: the Bordeaux climbing-robot workshop (29 Oct–1 Nov) starts Wednesday — no published registration cut-off, so an email today is the whole decision. Five items fall on next Friday, 31 Oct: Défi Aérospatial Étudiant, EdenSpark #4, RGDC Jam, Slapjam #2, and the quantum-hackathon check-back.

Events

Statuses only today — nothing new cleared the bar on a quiet Sunday: the climbing-robots site is unchanged (the simulator is still listed as T.B.D., a small risk with hackathon kick-off four days away; the registration row has grown to eight teams), and the quantum-hackathon sign-up page remains live with the registration question unresolved.

Game jams

Quiet again on the cash-jam front; the December wave (Pixel Forge 28 Nov start, Uplifting 1–14 Dec) is the next thing worth watching, and both are already tracked.

AI

  • The M3.1 Flash “open weights” story resolved into its honest shape. vLLM added day-0 serving with native NVFP4 KV cache and MoE weights plus DSpark speculative decoding, and owns the headline numbers: drawing 724.9 tok/s per user at the latency-sensitive end on Rubin (418.9 on GB300, 331 on AMD’s MI355X) on the SemiAnalysis AgentX benchmark. Community GGUF lists Q4 at ~249 GB — 4× DGX Spark or 2× Mac Studio 512 GB territory — and llama.cpp does not implement MiniMax Sparse Attention yet, so local builds fall back to dense attention and lose the 1M-context edge; Ollama’s tag is cloud-only. Friday’s “put it on a 2×24 GB card” plan is dead on these numbers: this is a datacenter-affordability model.
  • One useful independent datapoint surfaced amid the vendor benchmarks: the Ollama Cloud endpoint sustains ~104 tok/s per user live-benchmarked — right in the usable-coding-assistant band, at the promised price.
  • Mistral’s own Large 4 weights remain the notable open promise for the back half of the month. The 93%-hallucination claim from independent testers is still unverified either way.

Geopolitics

  • The Houthi campaign found Saudi Arabia’s bypass artery. Strikes on Aramco’s Yanbu and Rabigh sites and claimed hits on the East-West pipeline — which skirts the Strait of Hormuz — plus renewed warnings at Riyadh’s airport explain a lot: the EU–GCC summit was postponed over exactly this escalation, turning Friday’s correction into a structural story.
  • Canada and the EU hold a summit in Montréal on 29–30 Oct, announced today — the first non-European member of the EU’s SAFE initiative deepening a partnership built on energy, critical minerals, space, AI and quantum. Another axis of the sanctions-era realignment.
  • Ukraine diplomacy splits in two: Zelenskyy offered to freeze combat along current frontlines while refusing any 25% territorial concession, as Europe shortlists five names (Meloni, Stubb, Pavel, Frederiksen, Støre) for a joint negotiator — without yet holding the unified lever the job would need. Starshield speeds are reported restored in Ukraine.
  • A full ballot weekend: Bulgarians elect a president from 27 candidates and Serbia holds snap parliamentary elections — both encoding how the sanctions-war continent’s politics are aligning; in France, ~410,000 protested across the country on Saturday over living costs.

Project ideas

  • Yesterday’s consumer-GPU bench idea dies properly — turn it into a better experiment. With no MSA in llama.cpp, the open question is what dense-attention fallback actually costs at long context: build a small perplexity/recall-vs-context benchmark (32K–512K tokens) comparing community GGUF against the vLLM NVFP4 checkpoint, and you directly measure the “sparse attention tax” — and, incidentally, probe ground near that 93% hallucination claim. Smallest step: pull Unsloth’s Q4 build, log PPL at 8K/32K/128K.
  • The “paper trail” claim-checking CLI stands (openAlex → ranked sources → markdown citations); no change since yesterday.
