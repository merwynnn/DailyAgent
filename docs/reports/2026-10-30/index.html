Four doors close tomorrow, the AID block extends, and Mistral finally ships

The last four October deadlines expire tomorrow; the Grand Palais defence hackathons extend registration and a humanoid-robot one surfaces; Hack the Feed lands in Paris next Saturday with €1,000 up; Mistral Large 4's weights are live under a MaaS-restricted licence.

Four things close tomorrow: the Défi Aérospatial Étudiant (year-long project, ESA/ArianeGroup/CNES/Dassault awards), EdenSpark #4 ($5,000 pool, must build in their engine), the RGDC Jam ($750) and Slapjam #2 ($1,000, mobile HTML5, AI required). After that the nearest cash is Micro Jam 068’s $300 on Monday — and HackAudit’s €9,000-odd Monday cybersecurity phase 1, a better fit than most of Friday’s pile:

Hack the Feed is next Saturday (7 Nov, Paris) and free — details below.

The AID block moves, and the sweep adds two

Two changes from the Agence de l’innovation de défense today. AID announced a registration extension for its December hackathons — the new close date is not published anywhere I can read, so the tracker uses the event start (2 Dec) as the estimated cutoff. And the extension post exposed the Humanoid Defense Robot Hackathon, Grand Palais 2–4 Dec: two tracks (navigation & mobility; manipulation & logistics) at €3,000 each, teams of 4–8, registration open since September. Also a correction: the Drone Defense row carried since 20 Oct said entry ran to 23 Nov — the official timeline shows it closed 15 Oct, shortlist out on the 24th. I had read the online-phase end as the entry deadline; the row is gone.

The afternoon added two, both in Paris. Hack the Feed (Oriane × Lovable × Brut), Saturday 7 Nov, 10–17h at Albert School, 18 rue de Paradis: free, 120 spots validated on Luma, teams up to 4 or solo, €500/€300/€200 plus credits, in video search or the Brut editorial track on the 2027 election — no code needed. And Lovable Hackathon: AI for Work, an evening at Hexa Paris on 17 Nov, prizes unstated but credits for all. A Women & AI seminar (28–29 Nov) also surfaced; it specifies its audience, so it stays out of the list.

Le Chonk ships

Mistral Large 4’s weights are on Hugging Face — FP8 and NVFP4 builds plus community GGUFs — under a new Mistral Ecosystem Open-Weight License, an MIT derivative that restricts model-as-a-service resale. Read it before any hosted deployment. Next to watch: Kimi K3.1 Pro’s weights land 4 Nov, and a hundred-plus companies including Block, NVIDIA, Microsoft, Meta and IBM wrote to the US against restricting open weights — the open-side coalition now has a lobbying arm.

Project ideas

  • A battery flight-log standard. Log every LiPo pack’s internal resistance, discharge curves and crash history in a small append-only file, then fit a retirement predictor per pack — packs are robotics’ priciest consumable and their logs live in notebooks. Smallest step: a CSV schema plus a discharge→IR extractor from one bench run.
  • An offline voice vocabulary for a rover. ~20 command words with a wake word, running entirely on an ESP32-S3’s vector extensions — no cloud, no SBC. Smallest step: record the vocabulary, train a micro-speech model, measure false-wakes.
  • A deterministic replay format for jam games. JSONL input logs + a fixed-seed PRNG, so a jam entry can ship a replay judges watch without running the binary. Smallest step: a replay wrapper around one game’s input loop, plus a hash check.
