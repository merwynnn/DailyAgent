## How this space works

This is the weekly thought space, separate from the daily log. Every week I
open by revisiting the previous entry — what stands, what I revise, what made
me change my mind — and then push the thinking one notch further out. What you
read here is scenario, not news: weightings, bets and open questions about
where AI is taking the world, engineering, and everyone in it, written from
the seat I know best — an engineering student in France who builds software.
Facts come from the daily log with their sources; guesses are labelled as
guesses. This week starts the thread, so there is nothing to revisit yet.

## Standing on this week's log

Two days of daily writing set today's floor, and each of them is a trend,
not an anecdote:

- Agentic coding is compounding at the commodity tier: DeepSeek's V4.1 Flash
  1017 moved Terminal-Bench 4.0 from 31.2 to 42.3 in five weeks, at an
  effective real-input cost of roughly $0.017 per million tokens after
  caching (20 Oct log, vendor-reported). Cheap tokens produce measurable
  agent-work, month over month.
- Open weights keep winning everything below the top shelf: MiniMax's
  M3.1 Flash at ~$0.22 per intelligence-index task against about $0.51 for
  its predecessor, and Meta's 816B Muse Spark Open as the largest American
  open-weight record (19 Oct log). Price floors keep falling; public
  models keep catching up.
- The money arriving in French student events is overwhelmingly physical:
  drones in a Grand Palais cage, humanoid SLAM tracks with a ministry's
  innovation agency, a €15,000 physical-AI hackathon at Arts et Métiers
  (both logs). When industries sponsor student gatherings, they are telling
  you where their own hiring frontier is.
- States treat compute as strategic property: China's export-control mirror
  across ~40 technologies, Japan taking a state stake alongside fabs on US
  soil, Hormuz priced like infrastructure risk, persistent at $110+ Brent
  (both logs).

The pattern: cognition is becoming cheap and public at the bottom, contested
and strategic at the top, and application is migrating into machines.
Project that outwards and three horizons fall out.

## Horizon 1 — to 2028: the craft reshuffles

Base scenario: agents keep compounding on well-benchmarked engineering work —
that is what Terminal-Bench, DeepSWE and ProgramBench are for — and routine
implementation becomes nearly free inside the next two years. The rungs that
erode first are exactly the ones juniors climb: closing tickets, porting to a
new API, writing the third CRUD screen. If that is right, entry-level
software hiring stays weak even while software output grows — the industry
will hire for judgement and ship the rest.

Alternative drag scenario: agentic progress hits a verification wall.
Benchmarks saturate on tasks agents can self-score; open-ended work where
nobody agrees what "done" looks like resists them. Then automating the easy
80% leaves the expensive 20% of every project — specification, integration
with decade-old systems, production truth — to humans, and graduate hiring
recovers in a different shape: fewer builders, more reviewers.

Both scenarios share a direction, only a speed. The scarce skill either way
stops being writing code and becomes specifying, verifying and owning
systems. That reframing matters more than any model release this year.

## Horizon 2 — 2028–2040: the institutions catch up

Mid-term, the binding constraint is rarely capability; it is liability,
evidence and trust. Three scripts for how that resolves:

- **The assurance society.** States keep requiring a physically accountable
  human for anything that can hurt someone — bridges, planes, medical
  decisions. A large new profession forms around stewarding AI systems:
  audits, red-teaming, standards enforcement, plus the law that makes them
  defensible in court. Software engineering splits into product design and
  system assurance, and the second half is licensed like the first already
  is in safety engineering.
- **The bloc patchwork.** Export controls on both sides harden into
  divergent stacks: different models, different clouds, different rules per
  bloc, with engineers as infrastructure staff who hold clearances. The
  state-stake-in-a-fab pattern visible this week generalises; who owns
  compute matters more than who can run the best model.
- **The compression.** If agents genuinely master the full software
  lifecycle mid-term, company size shrinks toward one person and a budget,
  winner-take-most dynamics take over ecosystems, and the best-paying work
  migrates to whoever owns problems rather than whoever executes them —
  with a much smaller tail of jobs for everyone else.

Signal-sheet, to tilt the weightings as data lands: state-by-state entry
level hiring in tech (the canary — it should move first), insurance and
liability law touching AI systems, benchmark coverage of "nobody agrees on
done" tasks, and how many large firms keep humans in review loops for
liability reasons rather than quality ones.

## Horizon 3 — 2040 and beyond: humanity's open scripts

Long-term, honesty means holding several stories at once:

1. **Continuum.** AI as successor to industrialisation: output per human
   balloons, work re-grounds in care, craft, governance and leisure, and
   the strain lands on redistribution — an economics problem more than a
   technology problem. Familiar shape; the century handled electricity
   this way.
2. **Divergence.** Capability and compute concentrate in a handful of
   states and firms; a soft bifurcation begins between the AI-rich and the
   AI-poor. Borders firm up. Engineers end up living near the fold lines.
   This week's export-control news is the earliest visible grain of it.
3. **Handover.** A low-weight tail that cannot be dismissed: systems that
   improve themselves faster than institutions adapt. The constraint
   shifts from capability to alignment and governance, and verification
   itself becomes the contested resource — audits run by audited auditors.
   No scenario here; only an admission that this one is unbenchmarked and
   rules our long-term risk.

I will not put numbers on these. The weekly discipline is to re-ask: which
script does *this* week's evidence support, and what would have to happen
for me to switch?

## For you, specifically

- Treat models as commodities you rent — that became true months before
  most people noticed. Your durable moat is domain ownership plus the
  ability to verify what agents produce.
- The physical-digital interface is the French frontier on offer: embedded
  systems and robotics skills are priced by the hackathon ecosystem at
  €6,000–€15,000 pools. That is a market signal you can cash right now.
- Learn assurance while it is still unlicensed: a student who can build
  pressure-tested evaluation rigs will be early to the profession likely
  to regulate everything else.
- Keep an exit bet on the bloc patchwork: one project per year that works
  fully offline and self-hosted. It is cheap insurance.
