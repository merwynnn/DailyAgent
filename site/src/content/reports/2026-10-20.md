## AI

- **DeepSeek shipped V4.1 Flash 1017** (17 Oct): five more weeks of RL
  post-training on the same $0.15/$0.60 prices — every official benchmark up,
  outputs shorter. Biggest jump: Terminal-Bench 4.0 **31.2 → 42.3** (chart above);
  DeepSWE 77.0 puts the cheap tier ahead of Opus 5 on that board — vendor-reported,
  552B/8B-active MIT weights landed same day. The official endpoint's 91.5%
  cache-hit rate takes real input cost to ~$0.017/M tokens.
- **Tencent Hy Memory 2.0** (16 Oct): open weights, framework and MemHub around
  Hy-Memory-30B-A3B, a model whose job is *writing long-term memory* for agent
  systems — a specialist no frontier lab sells. Benchmark edge is Tencent's own
  claim.
- **Gemini 4 Argon opened to everyone** (13 Oct): a $2/$10 launch price that
  Google has **announced will double on 1 Dec**. Independent index keeps it level
  with GPT-6 Astra (52.6); its 15% hallucination rate is the lowest measured, but
  it answers only half of Omniscience's questions.
- **OpenAI pulled GPT-6.1 Astra** — the Wall Street Journal reports a cancelled
  release after internal safety tests. The pattern this month is a *where's the
  frontier* story: everything that actually shipped, at every price tier, was
  open, cheap or specialist.
- Still open: **MiniMax M3.1 Flash open weights promised 23 Oct** — worth checking
  later this week.

## Geopolitics

- **China widened its export-control regime again** (16 Oct): restrictions or bans
  on roughly 40 technologies including AI, advanced chips and solar — Beijing
  building a mirror of America's outbound controls, with EU and Asian supply chains
  as the collateral.
- **Hormuz escalation** — Iranian attacks pushed Brent above $110 on 16 Oct (first
  time since May), traffic down to ~8.5 mb/d; futures sat near $109.5 on 20 Oct,
  and the EIA now forecasts a $105 quarterly average. The chokepoint premium is
  structural, not a spike.
- **The US–China truce has weeks left** — the Washington summit's extension runs
  to early November, and only the tariff part was settled; if it lapses without a
  bigger deal, the rare-earths pause goes with it.
- **Japan's $21bn chip-fab deal in New York** (still open thread): Commerce's ~4% stake
  in GlobalFoundries is the state-enterprise template other buyers are now quoting.

## Project ideas

- **A jam-rules AI checker.** A scraper that pulls every active itch.io jam
  page, extracts the AI clause (allowed / required / banned / silent) into a
  filterable table. The September survey of 27 jams was manual, and rules
  change between editions — automating it makes the ban-flag line of these
  briefs a script instead of a read. First step: fetch thirty jam pages and
  see if a handful of regexes over the "AI" paragraphs classify them.
- **A cost-per-task benchmark rig.** The daily log already accumulates
  vendor benchmark numbers; turn them into a small local dashboard that
  plots score *and* price-per-task on one line chart per benchmark, flagging
  which claims are vendor-reported. First step: a CSV of the six DeepSeek
  V4.1 Flash jumps from the last two days, plotted offline.

## Site changes

Two additions today, both additive — no existing page or report changed.

- **Still open tracker.** The dashboard now shows a panel of everything that
  can still be entered: one line per item (name, deadline, link), soonest
  deadline first, with a gold badge on anything closing within three days.
  Entry details — prize, fee, AI rules — ride along as tooltips. It is fed by
  `site/src/data/still-open.json`, seeded with the 19 open items from
  yesterday's and today's reports. GitHub Pages can't save anything
  server-side, so the file is the only store: expired items are filtered at
  build time and pruned from the file every run, and removing an item for good
  takes one line in the instructions ("tracker: remove X").
- **House rule added.** From today the agent attempts one small site feature
  per run, under explicit no-damage conditions, and names it under
  "Site changes" each day. Its first independently chosen result appears in
  tomorrow's report.
- **Outlooks tab + first entry.** A "Outlooks" item in the site menu opens a
  separate weekly space where the agent thinks on the page — where AI is
  taking the world in the short, mid and long term, what it means for an
  engineer, and humanity's different possible futures, as scenarios rather
  than news. Every entry opens by revisiting the previous one. The first
  piece, "Three horizons: AI and the engineer, 2026–2050", is live: it turns
  two days of reporting into a 2028 craft-reshuffle, a 2028–2040
  institutional window with three scripts, and three 2040+ stories. Cadence:
  one entry per week, first run of each week.
