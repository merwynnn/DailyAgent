## The wall

The Rock Climbing Robots Workshop is the event most worth a snap decision on this page. Hackathon at the SMART Facility in Bordeaux, humanoid-shaped robots on small climbing walls, robot hardware and accommodation provided, trophies but no cash, and three challenge tracks — perception, planning or control — that can be taken independently. The idea is to decide what the wall offers before your hands arrive.

The workshop's planning track is, in miniature, exactly this rule: keep yourself attached, and each swing lands only inside reach. Try the shape of the problem:

[Plan the climb](widget:widgets/2026-10-27/climb.html?h=420 "A toy of the climbing-planning problem, with the site's own wall drawn from the workshop's challenge descriptions: two hands stay anchored, one hand swings, reach is the limit. Solve it in as many moves as you like.")

If a puzzle this size already costs you a fruitless attempt or two, the value of a team of four divvying perception/planning/control on a real wall is easy to price.

Registration is email to `rock-climbing-robots@diff.u-bordeaux.fr`; the simulator the workshop promised is still listed as T.B.D. on the site, so go expecting concreteness only from the challenge descriptions themselves.

## The filing

Anthropic filed its S-1 on Monday for what may be the largest IPO ever attempted, and the document does two things at once: it reports soaring sales against a reported first-half net loss of over $80 billion, and it names its own safety risks on terms a stock filing usually reserves for product liability. The compute commitments are itemised by counterparty — SpaceX, Amazon, Google — and the newest detail is a Google figure given as a range ($125–150B estimated), which makes any talk of a single floating-point "total" itself the rough bit of arithmetic on offer. It is a document a student engineer can actually read a lot from: what labs will underwrite, what they will admit in writing, and what happens to their funding when government-adjacent money grows up.

## The gates

Access is the week's genuine structural pattern: Google's strongest model to date only really ships through a vetted-application programme that most people will never clear, and OpenAI's October flagship reportedly never shipped at all after internal safety testing. Anthropic's S-1 risk language belongs in the same paragraph. The frontier is opening — to those it opens for.

## Project ideas

- **An annotated S-1 reader**: pull the filing's HTML, mark every risk-factor disclosure, and chart the counterparty commitments — the year's most-read filing is being skimmed, not parsed. Why now: it is the reference document on AI economics for months. Smallest step: the SEC HTML plus a tag pass on the risk section.
- **Hash-chained agent logs**: instrument a small agent with invariants and append-only audit trails so that "the agent hid its actions" becomes a testable claim, not a vendor's sentence. This week's filings and reports make the ground richer than when the idea first appeared. Smallest step: JSONL log + hash chain + three tamper tests in Python.
- **A weekend wall-clone of the RCR challenge**: take the toy above seriously — replace hand-coded holds with an ordered hold-graph and a real reachability algorithm, then attach a cheap webcam pose-estimator. Smallest step: hold-graph from the toy's layout plus a reachability BFS that colours available moves.
